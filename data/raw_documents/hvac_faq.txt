Q: Why is my air conditioner blowing warm air?
A: This is usually caused by low refrigerant levels, a dirty air filter restricting airflow, or a failing compressor. Start by checking and replacing the air filter. If the problem persists, a technician should check refrigerant levels, as low refrigerant often indicates a leak that needs professional repair.

Q: How often should I change my HVAC air filter?
A: Standard 1-inch filters should be replaced every 30-90 days depending on usage, pets, and air quality. Homes with pets or allergy sufferers should change filters closer to every 30 days. Thicker 4-5 inch media filters can last 6-12 months.

Q: What does it mean when my furnace is short cycling?
A: Short cycling (turning on and off frequently) is often caused by an oversized furnace, a dirty flame sensor, a clogged filter, or a malfunctioning thermostat. It increases wear on the system and should be diagnosed by a technician if changing the filter doesn't resolve it.

Q: How much does it cost to replace an HVAC system?
A: A full system replacement typically ranges from $5,000 to $12,000 depending on system size, efficiency rating (SEER), and home square footage. We offer free in-home estimates before any work begins.

Q: What is a SEER rating and why does it matter?
A: SEER (Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio) measures cooling efficiency. Higher SEER ratings mean lower energy bills. As of 2023, minimum SEER requirements increased to 14-15 depending on region. Upgrading from an old SEER 10 unit to a SEER 16+ unit can cut cooling costs significantly.

Q: My thermostat screen is blank, what should I check?
A: First check if it's battery-powered and replace the batteries. If hardwired, check the circuit breaker for the HVAC system. If neither resolves it, there may be a wiring issue that requires a technician.

Q: Why is there ice building up on my AC unit?
A: Ice buildup is usually caused by restricted airflow (dirty filter or blocked vents), low refrigerant, or a failing blower motor. Turn the system off and let it thaw, then check the filter. If ice returns quickly, schedule a technician visit.

Q: Why won't my furnace turn on at all?
A: Common causes include a tripped circuit breaker, a thermostat set incorrectly or with dead batteries, a closed gas valve, or a safety switch that's been triggered by a dirty filter. Check the thermostat and breaker first before calling for service.

Q: My HVAC system is making strange noises, what could it be?
A: Banging or clanking often points to a loose part or failing motor bearing. Squealing usually means a worn belt or motor issue. Hissing can indicate a refrigerant leak. Any new or loud noise is worth having inspected before it causes further damage.

Q: How do I reset the circuit breaker for my HVAC system?
A: Locate your home's electrical panel, find the breaker labeled for the furnace or AC (often labeled HVAC, AC, or Furnace), switch it fully to OFF, wait 30 seconds, then switch it back ON. If it trips again immediately, do not keep resetting it - call a technician, as repeated tripping indicates an electrical fault.

Q: What's the difference between a heat pump and a furnace?
A: A furnace generates heat by burning fuel or using electric resistance. A heat pump instead moves existing heat from outside air into your home (and can reverse to cool in summer), making it more energy-efficient in moderate climates but less effective in extreme cold without a backup heat source.

Q: Do you install ductless mini-split systems?
A: Yes, we install ductless mini-splits for additions, garages, or homes without existing ductwork. They offer room-by-room temperature control and are typically more efficient than extending ductwork to a new space.

Q: How can I improve the indoor air quality in my home?
A: Options include upgrading to a higher-MERV-rated filter, adding a whole-home air purifier or UV light system, ensuring proper ventilation, and scheduling regular duct cleaning. We can recommend the right combination based on your home and any allergy concerns.

Q: Why does my house feel too humid even with the AC running?
A: An oversized AC unit can cool the air quickly without running long enough to remove humidity, since dehumidification happens during longer run cycles. A dirty filter or low refrigerant can also reduce moisture removal. A whole-home dehumidifier can help if the unit itself is properly sized.

Q: Why is my house too dry in the winter?
A: Forced-air heating systems circulate air without adding moisture, and cold outdoor air holds less humidity to begin with. A whole-home humidifier installed on your furnace can maintain comfortable humidity levels through the winter.

Q: How often should my air ducts be cleaned?
A: Most homes benefit from duct cleaning every 3-5 years, though homes with pets, recent renovations, or visible dust buildup at vents may need it sooner.

Q: Should I have a carbon monoxide detector near my furnace?
A: Yes, a CO detector near sleeping areas and near any fuel-burning appliance like a furnace is strongly recommended and required by code in many areas, since a cracked heat exchanger can leak carbon monoxide without any smell.

Q: Why does my AC keep turning on and off frequently in the summer?
A: This short cycling in cooling mode is often due to an oversized unit, a refrigerant issue, a dirty condenser coil, or a thermostat placed somewhere that doesn't reflect the room's actual temperature (like near a vent or in direct sun).

Q: What size AC unit do I need for my home?
A: Sizing depends on square footage, ceiling height, insulation, window count and orientation, and local climate - not just square footage alone. An oversized unit short-cycles and struggles with humidity; an undersized one runs constantly. We calculate proper sizing with a load calculation during your estimate.

Q: Are there rebates available for energy-efficient systems?
A: Yes, many high-efficiency systems qualify for manufacturer rebates and sometimes local utility or government rebates. Availability changes frequently, so ask during your estimate and we'll check current programs in your area.

Q: What is a UV light air purifier and is it worth adding?
A: A UV light system installed in your ductwork helps neutralize mold, bacteria, and some airborne viruses as air passes through. It's most worth adding for households with allergy or respiratory concerns, or homes with a history of mold in the ductwork.

Q: What is a zoned HVAC system?
A: Zoning uses dampers in the ductwork and multiple thermostats to let different areas of your home be heated or cooled independently, useful for multi-story homes or additions that run hotter or colder than the rest of the house.

Q: Why is my heat pump blowing cold air when it's set to heat?
A: A brief period of cooler air during defrost cycles in cold weather is normal. If it's consistently cold, causes include low refrigerant, a reversing valve stuck in cooling mode, or a failing component - this needs a technician diagnosis.

Q: How important is an annual tune-up if my system seems fine?
A: Regular tune-ups catch small issues (worn belts, low refrigerant, dirty coils) before they cause a breakdown or a bigger repair bill, and they help maintain your system's efficiency and manufacturer warranty requirements.

Q: What are signs I need to replace my thermostat?
A: Inconsistent temperatures room to room, a thermostat that doesn't hold its setting, a system that won't respond to changes, or a screen that's dim or unresponsive even with fresh batteries are all signs it may be time to replace it.

Q: There's water leaking from my indoor unit, what should I do?
A: This usually means a clogged condensate drain line. Turn off the system to prevent water damage and check if the drain pan is full. If you can't clear the clog yourself, a technician can clear the line and check for a secondary cause like a frozen coil that's now thawing.

Q: I smell gas near my furnace, what should I do?
A: Leave the house immediately, do not turn on any lights or switches, and call your gas utility's emergency line and us from outside the home. Never attempt to locate or fix a gas leak yourself.

Q: There's a strange smell when my AC first turns on, is that normal?
A: A brief musty smell can be normal if the system has been off a while, especially from dust in the ducts. A persistent burning smell is not normal and could indicate an electrical or motor issue - turn the system off and call for service if the smell continues.
